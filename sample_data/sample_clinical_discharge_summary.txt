HOSPITAL COURSE & CLINICAL SUMMARY:
The patient is a 62-year-old male who presented to the emergency department with a 4-day history of productive cough with purulent yellow-green sputum, fever peaking at 38.9°C (102°F), pleuritic right-sided chest pain, and severe exertional dyspnea. Initial room air arterial oxygen saturation was 88%, which corrected to 94% on 3 liters nasal cannula oxygen. 

Chest radiography and thoracic CT revealed right lower lobe alveolar consolidation and air bronchograms consistent with lobar pneumonia. Initial laboratory evaluation demonstrated leukocytosis with white blood cell count of 15,800/mcL and elevated C-reactive protein (CRP) of 84 mg/L. Blood and sputum cultures were collected.

The patient was admitted to the inpatient medical ward and started on empiric intravenous antibiotic therapy consisting of Ceftriaxone 1 gram IV every 24 hours plus Azithromycin 500 mg IV every 24 hours. Bronchodilator therapy with nebulized Albuterol and Ipratropium bromide was administered every 6 hours. Systemic corticosteroids with oral Prednisone 40 mg daily were given for 5 days for COPD exacerbation.

Over 96 hours, the patient exhibited marked clinical improvement. Fever subsided by hospital day 3, leukocytosis trended down to 7,200/mcL, and supplemental oxygen was successfully weaned to room air with resting SpO2 stable at 95%. Sputum gram stain showed gram-positive diplococci consistent with Streptococcus pneumoniae, sensitive to fluoroquinolones.
